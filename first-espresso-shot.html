How to Pull Your First Espresso Shot

8 min read From cold machine to first sip

Pulling your first espresso shot can feel intimidating. There are variables to track, timers to watch, and a puck of coffee that demands more precision than a french press. But the process itself is straightforward once you've done it a couple of times. This guide walks you through every step — slowly and clearly.

Don't worry about perfection on shot one. The goal is to understand the process. Dialing in comes later.

01

What you need before you start

Have everything in place before you turn the machine on. Scrambling mid-brew leads to mistakes.

  • —

    Espresso machine

    Any machine capable of 9 bars of pressure. Semi-automatic machines give you the most control as a beginner.

  • —

    Grinder

    Set to a fine espresso grind. If you're unsure where to start, set it to the finest setting and work coarser from there.

  • —

    Scale

    You need to weigh your dose (coffee in) and yield (espresso out). A basic kitchen scale accurate to 0.1g works fine.

  • —

    Timer

    Your phone stopwatch is fine. Some scales have a built-in timer, which is handy.

  • —

    Fresh whole beans

    Roasted within the last 2–5 weeks. Grind them immediately before brewing — never use pre-ground for espresso.

  • —

    A small cup or shot glass

    Place it on your scale under the portafilter spout.

02

Warm up your machine properly

Espresso is brewed at around 200°F (93°C). A cold machine gives you inconsistent, under-extracted shots no matter how perfect everything else is. Most home machines need 15–30 minutes to reach stable brewing temperature — check your machine's manual for the specific recommendation.

Once warmed up, run a "blank shot" — pull a shot with no coffee in the portafilter. This flushes the group head, brings it up to temperature, and clears any stale water sitting in the lines. Discard the water and dry the portafilter basket before grinding.

Pro tip

If your machine has a steam wand, turn it on and let it warm up too. Even if you're not frothing milk today, a fully heated machine is a stable machine.

03

Grind and dose your coffee

Place your portafilter basket on the scale and tare it to zero. Grind your coffee directly into the basket, targeting 18 grams for a standard double shot. Stop grinding when you hit your target weight.

The grind should look and feel like fine table salt — finer than drip coffee, but not quite a powder. If you're not sure, you'll adjust based on how your shot flows in the next step.

Why 18g?

Most double-shot baskets are designed for 16–18g. Starting at 18g gives you a solid foundation. Once you're pulling consistent shots, you can experiment with different doses to change the flavor profile.

04

Distribute and tamp

This step has more impact than most beginners expect. An uneven puck means water finds a path of least resistance and "channels" — blasting through a weak spot while the rest of the coffee under-extracts.

  • 1

    Level the grounds

    Gently tap the side of the portafilter to settle the grounds, then use your finger to sweep across the top and even out any peaks or hollows. You want a flat, level surface before tamping.

  • 2

    Tamp straight down

    Place the tamper on the grounds and press straight down with firm, even pressure — around 30 lbs (14 kg). The key word is straight. A tilted tamp creates an uneven puck thickness and guarantees channeling.

  • 3

    Check the surface

    The tamped puck should look smooth and flat, sitting a few millimeters below the rim of the basket. Give the tamper a slight twist at the end if you like — it polishes the surface but doesn't materially affect extraction.

05

Lock in and pull the shot

Wipe the group head gasket with a damp cloth to clear any old grounds. Lock the portafilter into the group head with a firm twist — it should seat snugly with no wobble. Place your cup on the scale under the spout and tare it to zero.

Start the shot and your timer at the same moment. Watch the scale display as espresso flows into the cup. A healthy shot starts as a slow drip, builds into two thin streams, then merges into a single syrupy flow with a reddish-brown color.

Stop the shot when your scale reads 36g — that's a 1:2 ratio (18g in, 36g out). Note the time.

Target window

You want to hit 36g in 25–35 seconds. If you hit it much faster, the shot is running too fast (under-extracted — grind finer next time). Much slower, and it's over-extracting (grind coarser). The time is feedback, not the goal — the yield is the goal.

06

Taste and evaluate

Drink the shot while it's hot. Here's how to read what you're tasting:

Sour or sharp?

Under-extracted. The bright acids dominate before the sweetness has a chance to develop. Grind finer to slow the shot and increase extraction.

Bitter or harsh?

Over-extracted. Too many compounds have been pulled from the coffee. Grind coarser to speed things up and reduce extraction.

Sweet, balanced, complex?

You're in the zone. Note your grind setting, dose, yield, and time — that's your recipe. Repeat it tomorrow.

Expectation check

Your first shot will probably not be great. That is completely normal. Most people pull 3–10 shots before landing on something they'd call excellent. Each shot teaches you something. Treat the coffee as tuition — it's worth it.
