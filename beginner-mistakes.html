9 Common Beginner Espresso Mistakes

9 min read Fix these and your shots will improve overnight

Most espresso problems aren't about gear. They're about a handful of easily avoidable mistakes that most beginners make — often without realizing it. Here are the nine most common ones, why they matter, and exactly how to fix them.

Mistake 01

Using stale coffee

This is the single biggest mistake beginners make, and the hardest to spot because stale coffee doesn't look wrong. Coffee goes stale fast — whole beans start losing quality after 4–6 weeks post-roast, and ground coffee degrades within minutes. Old coffee produces flat, lifeless shots with no crema and a hollow, papery taste.

Check the roast date on your bag, not the best-by date. You want beans roasted within the last 2–5 weeks. If a bag doesn't have a roast date printed on it, that's a red flag.

The fix: Buy from a local roaster or a subscription that ships fresh. Look for the roast date, not the "best by" date.
Mistake 02

Not preheating the machine

Espresso is brewed at around 200°F (93°C). If your machine hasn't reached stable temperature, every shot will be under-extracted — tasting sour and thin regardless of how good your grind and technique are. Most home machines need 15–30 minutes to fully heat up.

Beyond the machine body, the portafilter and group head also need to be warm. A cold portafilter will drop the brewing temperature significantly. Run a blank shot (water only) through the group head after warmup to pre-heat everything before loading your coffee.

The fix: Turn your machine on before you do anything else. Run a blank shot through the group head before pulling your first real shot.
Mistake 03

Not weighing dose and yield

Eyeballing your coffee dose introduces enough variation to make your shots wildly inconsistent from day to day. A gram or two difference in dose changes shot time significantly. Without a scale, you're guessing — and you can't dial in a guess.

Weigh both your dose (coffee in, typically 18g) and your yield (espresso out, typically 36g). A basic kitchen scale accurate to 0.1g costs $15–$25 and is the best cheap upgrade in espresso.

The fix: Weigh everything. Dose in grams, yield in grams. Stop the shot by weight, not by time or by eye.
Mistake 04

Tamping at an angle

A crooked tamp creates an uneven puck — thicker on one side, thinner on the other. Water always finds the path of least resistance, so it blasts through the thin side while the thick side barely extracts. The result is "channeling": a shot that tastes simultaneously bitter and sour, with no way to fix it mid-brew.

Tamp straight down, perpendicular to the basket. Use the rim of the portafilter as a visual guide. Consistent 30 lbs of pressure is less important than the angle — a firm, level tamp at 20 lbs is better than a heavy, tilted one.

The fix: Focus on tamping straight, not tamping hard. Use a self-leveling tamper if consistency is a recurring issue.
Mistake 05

Changing two variables at once

When a shot tastes bad, the temptation is to fix everything simultaneously — adjust the grind, change the dose, alter the yield. But if you change multiple things and the next shot is better, you don't know which change helped. If it's worse, you don't know which one made it worse.

Dialing in is a process of controlled experiments. Change one variable, pull one shot, evaluate. Then decide what to change next.

The fix: One change at a time. Write down what you changed and what happened. Your shot log is your most useful tool.
Mistake 06

Using a grinder that can't grind fine enough

Not every grinder can produce grounds fine enough for espresso. Blade grinders, most manual grinders under $50, and many budget electric grinders don't have the range to hit espresso-fine grinds. If your shot runs in 10 seconds no matter how you adjust, your grinder has hit its limit.

For espresso, you need a burr grinder with an espresso setting — either a flat or conical burr model rated for espresso use. This is why the grinder is the most important purchase in your setup.

The fix: Verify your grinder is rated for espresso before buying. If you already own one that can't go fine enough, upgrading the grinder will do more for your shots than any other change.
Mistake 07

Skipping the distribution step

Coffee grounds don't fall evenly into the portafilter basket. You'll often get a mound in the center, clumps from static, or an uneven surface. Tamping over an uneven bed locks in all those inconsistencies — the tamp doesn't fix them, it seals them in place.

Before tamping, take a few seconds to distribute: tap the side of the portafilter gently, then use a finger or a distribution tool to level the surface. A WDT (Weiss Distribution Technique) tool — essentially a thin needle stirrer — breaks up clumps effectively and cheaply.

The fix: Always distribute before tamping. A level surface before the tamp is essential for an even extraction.
Mistake 08

Not purging the steam wand

After steaming milk, a small amount of milk gets drawn back into the steam wand tip. If you don't purge it immediately — by briefly opening the steam valve to blast it out — that milk dries inside the wand and eventually blocks it. It also re-introduces bacterial growth into the wand.

Purge the wand immediately after every steam, and wipe the tip with a damp cloth. If your wand is already partially blocked, soak the tip in hot water for a few minutes to soften the buildup.

The fix: Purge the steam wand and wipe it down after every use. Make it automatic — done before you even set down the pitcher.
Mistake 09

Neglecting machine cleaning

Coffee oils are acidic and go rancid. Every shot leaves a thin film on your group head, portafilter, and basket. Over days and weeks, that buildup gives your shots a stale, bitter undertone that no amount of grind adjustment will fix — because it's not your coffee, it's your dirty machine.

Rinse your portafilter basket after every session. Run a blank shot through the group head. Backflush with water weekly (or with espresso cleaning tablets monthly if your machine supports it). A clean machine is a consistent machine.

The fix: Rinse the portafilter basket after every use. Do a proper cleaning with espresso detergent at least once a month.
