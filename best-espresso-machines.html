Best Espresso Machines for Beginners

7 min read Tested picks from budget to serious

The machine is only half the equation — your grinder matters just as much — but choosing the right machine for your skill level and budget sets the ceiling on what's possible. The four picks below cover every starting point, from a first-timer on a tight budget to someone ready to invest in a serious setup from day one.

Every machine on this list is semi-automatic, uses a pump (not steam pressure), and is genuinely learnable without a barista background.

Before you buy

Don't skip the grinder. A great machine with a bad grinder will always produce mediocre espresso. If you're working with a limited budget, spend proportionally more on the grinder — it makes a bigger difference. See our grinder guide for where to start.

Our top pick

Best overall: Breville Bambino Plus

Breville Bambino Plus

~$499 on Amazon

The Bambino Plus is the closest thing to a no-compromise beginner machine. It heats up in 3 seconds via Breville's Thermojet system, uses a 54mm portafilter that produces espresso comparable to machines costing twice as much, and comes with an automatic steam wand that textures milk consistently — even before you've learned manual technique.

The compact footprint fits easily on most counters, and the single-dial interface is genuinely approachable. This is the machine we'd recommend to most beginners without hesitation.

Best slim design

Best compact: De'Longhi Dedica Arte

De'Longhi Dedica Arte

~$199 on Amazon

At just 6 inches wide, the Dedica Arte is one of the slimmest espresso machines available without sacrificing real espresso capability. It uses a proper 15-bar pump, heats up in around 40 seconds, and ships with De'Longhi's My LatteArt steam wand — a manual wand with a design that makes microfoam easier to achieve than a standard panarello.

This is the move if counter space is limited or the Bambino Plus is out of budget. The Dedica Arte punches well above its price, especially for milk drinks. The main trade-off is the 51mm portafilter, which limits basket options compared to the 54mm machines.

All-in-one pick

Best all-in-one: Breville Barista Express

Breville Barista Express

~$699 on Amazon

The Barista Express solves the "grinder + machine" problem in one unit with a built-in conical burr grinder. You load beans into the hopper, grind directly into the portafilter, and pull a shot — all without a separate grinder taking up counter space. For a true all-in-one setup, nothing at this price matches it.

The trade-off is flexibility: upgrading the grinder later means buying a new machine too. But for beginners who want quality results without building a multi-component setup, the Barista Express is a genuine shortcut to great espresso from day one.

Budget pick

Best budget: De'Longhi Stilosa

De'Longhi Stilosa

~$109 on Amazon

The Stilosa is the honest answer for anyone who wants to try real espresso without a large financial commitment. It uses a genuine 15-bar pump, a stainless steel boiler, and a manual steam wand — the fundamental components you need for proper espresso and milk drinks, at the lowest price point we'd feel comfortable recommending.

Limitations are real: it's slower, less consistent, and will frustrate you at the margins of dialing in. But as a learning machine or a low-risk entry point, it does the job. When you outgrow it, you'll know exactly what to upgrade to.
