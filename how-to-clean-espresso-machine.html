How to Clean and Descale Your Espresso Machine

8 min read Updated June 2026
The short version

Cleaning removes coffee oils and residue (do it daily and weekly). Descaling removes mineral scale from the water system (do it every 1–3 months). They're two different jobs with two different products — don't confuse them.

A 60-second daily rinse, a 10-minute weekly clean, and a monthly descale will keep your shots tasting clean and add years to your machine's life.

Maintenance is the most ignored part of home espresso — and it's quietly responsible for a huge share of "my machine is broken" and "my coffee suddenly tastes bad" problems. The good news is that the routine is simple and quick once you know what goes where. Here's the whole thing.

01

Cleaning vs. descaling — they're not the same

This trips up almost every beginner, so it's worth being clear:

Cleaning deals with coffee. Every shot leaves behind oils and fine grounds in the basket, group head, and (on machines with a 3-way valve) the internal pathways. Left alone, these oils go rancid and make every shot taste bitter. You clean with espresso machine cleaner (sometimes called Cafiza or "espresso detergent").

Descaling deals with water. Minerals in tap water — mostly calcium and magnesium — build up as hard scale inside the boiler and water lines. Scale slows heat-up, throws off temperature, and eventually clogs the machine. You descale with a descaling solution (citric-acid or lactic-acid based).

Using a descaler to clean coffee residue won't work, and using coffee cleaner to descale won't work. Two jobs, two products.

02

The daily routine (60 seconds)

Do this after every espresso session. It takes under a minute and prevents almost all buildup.

  1. 1
    Knock out the puck and rinse

    Dump the spent coffee puck, then rinse the portafilter and basket under hot water. Wipe them dry. Don't let coffee sit and dry in the basket.

  2. 2
    Flush the group head

    With the portafilter removed, run a quick "blank shot" of water through the group head for 2–3 seconds. This rinses grounds off the shower screen.

  3. 3
    Purge and wipe the steam wand

    If you steamed milk, purge the wand with a short steam burst and wipe it immediately with a damp cloth. Dried milk is hard to remove and breeds bacteria. This is the single most important daily habit.

  4. 4
    Empty the drip tray

    When it's full or at the end of the day, empty and rinse the drip tray so it doesn't get moldy.

03

The weekly clean (10 minutes)

Once a week, go deeper on the coffee residue.

  1. 1
    Soak the portafilter and basket

    Dissolve a scoop of espresso machine cleaner in hot water and soak the basket and portafilter (handle out of the water) for 15–20 minutes. You'll see the water turn brown. Scrub lightly, then rinse very well.

  2. 2
    Brush the group head and shower screen

    Use a group head brush to scrub around the shower screen and the rim of the group. Run a few blank shots to rinse the loosened residue out.

  3. 3
    Backflush (only if your machine has a 3-way valve)

    Machines like the Breville Barista Express and most prosumer machines can be backflushed: put a blind (solid) basket in the portafilter, add a little espresso cleaner, lock it in, and run the pump in short bursts. This forces cleaner back through the internal valve. Single-boiler machines without a 3-way valve (like the Bambino or Stilosa) should NOT be backflushed — check your manual if unsure.

Not sure if you can backflush?

If your machine came with a blind/solid basket in the box, it's designed to be backflushed. If it didn't, assume it can't be and skip that step — soaking and brushing are enough.

04

Descaling (every 1–3 months)

How often you descale depends on your water hardness and how much you use the machine. With hard tap water, monthly. With filtered or soft water, every 2–3 months. Many machines have a descale light that tells you when — but don't wait for it if you're on hard water.

  1. 1
    Mix the descaling solution

    Fill the water tank with descaling solution mixed to the ratio on the bottle (or follow your machine's built-in descale program). Use a proper espresso descaler — not vinegar, which can leave a lingering smell and isn't recommended by most manufacturers.

  2. 2
    Run it through every path

    Run the solution through the group head AND the steam wand and hot water spout, in batches, so it reaches all the internal water lines. Let it sit partway through if your machine's instructions say to.

  3. 3
    Flush thoroughly with fresh water

    This is critical. Refill the tank with clean water and run at least two full tanks through every path to rinse out all descaler. Any residue left behind will make your next few shots taste off. When in doubt, flush more.

Prevent scale in the first place

Using filtered water instead of hard tap water dramatically slows scale buildup — and improves how your espresso tastes. A simple water filter pitcher is the cheapest upgrade you can make to both your machine's lifespan and your coffee.

06

Mistakes to avoid

Using vinegar to descale. It can work in a pinch but the smell lingers, it's harsher on seals, and most manufacturers advise against it. A proper descaler is cheap.

Backflushing a machine that isn't built for it. Forcing water backward through a machine without a 3-way valve can damage it. Know your machine first.

Not rinsing enough after descaling. Descaler residue tastes terrible and can be mildly unpleasant to drink. Always over-flush.

Letting milk dry on the steam wand. The number-one source of funky-tasting milk and a sticky, clogged wand. Wipe it every single time.
