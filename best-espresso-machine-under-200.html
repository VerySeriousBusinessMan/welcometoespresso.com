Best Espresso Machine Under $200

7 min read Updated June 2026
The short version

You can absolutely make real espresso under $200 — but only from a couple of machines worth owning. The De'Longhi Dedica Arte (~$199) is the best machine you can buy right at the ceiling, and the De'Longhi Stilosa (~$109) is the best true-budget option if you want to spend as little as possible.

Avoid the no-name "20-bar" machines that flood Amazon at this price. More bars is not better, and most of them are pressurized pod-style units that can't grow with you.

Under $200 is the most crowded — and most misleading — price bracket in espresso. Search "espresso machine" and you'll get dozens of near-identical machines from brands you've never heard of, all advertising "20 bar!" or "15 bar!" pressure like it's a spec that matters. It isn't. What matters at this budget is whether the machine uses a real portafilter, has a usable steam wand, and comes from a brand that will still honor a warranty next year.

The honest truth: there are really only two or three machines under $200 we'd put our name behind. Here they are, plus exactly what you're trading away at this price and what to steer clear of.

The 3 best machines under $200

De'Longhi Dedica Arte

~$199 on Amazon

If your budget is right at $200, this is the machine to get. The Dedica Arte uses a real 15-bar pump, is only 6 inches wide, and ships with De'Longhi's My LatteArt steam wand — a manual wand that's noticeably easier to make microfoam with than the basic panarello wands on cheaper machines. It comes with both pressurized baskets (forgiving while you learn) and the ability to use non-pressurized ones later.

The trade-off: the 51mm portafilter is smaller than the 54–58mm standard, so accessories are more limited. But for milk drinks at this price, nothing else under $200 comes close.

03

What you give up under $200

It's worth being clear-eyed about this. A sub-$200 machine makes real espresso, but it's not the same experience as a $500 machine. Here's what the extra money buys at the next tier up:

No PID temperature control. Budget machines run at a roughly fixed temperature with simpler thermostats, so shot-to-shot consistency is a little looser.

Slower, smaller boilers. Expect 35–45 second heat-up and a short wait between pulling a shot and steaming milk. Machines like the Bambino heat in 3 seconds.

Pressurized baskets by default. These are forgiving — they force crema even with a so-so grind — but they cap how good your espresso can get. Most of these machines can take non-pressurized baskets later if you want to progress.

Simpler steam wands. The Dedica Arte's wand is the exception; most budget wands are panarello-style, which froth but don't produce true microfoam for latte art.

None of these are dealbreakers for a beginner. They're the honest cost of the price, and you can make genuinely good coffee despite all of them.

04

What to avoid at this price

"20-bar" and "15-bar" no-name machines. Pump pressure above 9 bar does nothing for espresso quality — 9 bar is the target, and every pump can hit the advertised number against a closed valve. It's a marketing number, not a quality signal. When an unknown brand leads with "20 BAR," it's usually all they have to sell.

Espresso/pod hybrids. Machines that take both ground coffee and pods almost always compromise the ground-coffee side. If you want espresso, buy an espresso machine; if you want pods, see our Nespresso vs. espresso comparison.

All-in-one machines with built-in grinders under $200. At this price, something has to give — and it's usually both the grinder and the machine. You're better off with a standalone budget machine and saving for a separate grinder later.

The grinder reality

Whatever machine you buy, a good grinder matters more than the machine itself. A $150 machine with a real burr grinder beats a $400 machine with pre-ground coffee every time. If you're on a tight total budget, pair one of these machines with a budget burr grinder rather than spending everything on the machine.

05

Should you save up instead?

If you can stretch to around $300, the Breville Bambino jumps you to a 54mm portafilter, 3-second heat-up, and PID temperature control — a real step up. And at ~$499 the Bambino Plus adds an automatic steam wand that textures milk for you.

But "save up" advice can be a trap. If $200 is your budget, a Dedica Arte you buy today and use every morning beats a Bambino you're still saving for in six months. These machines make real espresso. Start where you are — you can always upgrade later, and many people never feel the need to.
