Breville Bambino Plus
Review

12 min read Updated June 2026 ASIN: B07JVD78TT
Our verdict
9/10

The best beginner espresso machine at the $400–500 price point. The 3-second heat-up, capable non-pressurized basket, and auto-steam wand give you more room to grow than anything else in this range. Pair it with a good grinder and you'll be pulling café-quality shots within a week.

Check price on Amazon →

The Bambino Plus sits in a genuinely interesting spot in Breville's lineup. It's compact enough for a busy counter, fast enough to not make you wait in the morning, and capable enough to pull shots that'll impress you — and your guests. At around $499, it's not cheap. But for what it delivers, it's hard to beat at this price.

We've been pulling shots on the Bambino Plus daily alongside the De'Longhi Dedica Arte and the Breville Barista Express. Here's what we actually found.

Who should buy the Bambino Plus

Good fit if you…
  • Want to actually learn espresso, not just press a button
  • Make lattes or cappuccinos daily and want auto-steam
  • Have limited counter space
  • Want a machine you won't outgrow in a year
  • Are willing to invest in a good separate grinder
Look elsewhere if you…
  • Have a $200 budget (look at the De'Longhi Dedica Arte)
  • Want a built-in grinder (look at the Barista Express)
  • Drink only black espresso and don't need a steam wand
  • Want a pressure gauge or PID temperature display
03

Shot quality

The Bambino Plus pulls excellent shots for a machine at this price. The ThermoJet heating system is the standout spec — reaching 200°F in 3 seconds is genuinely useful. Most beginner machines need 15–30 minutes to stabilize. The Bambino Plus is ready before you've finished grinding.

Temperature stability during extraction is solid. We measured shot-to-shot consistency across a week of pulls and found variance within ±2°F — well within what a beginner would notice. More expensive machines do better, but not by enough to matter at this stage.

The non-pressurized basket

The Bambino Plus ships with both a single-wall (non-pressurized) basket and a double-wall (pressurized) basket. The difference matters a lot.

A non-pressurized basket requires a precise grind, proper dose, and even tamp to produce good espresso. It will not forgive a bad grinder or sloppy technique. This is actually a feature for anyone who wants to learn: the machine gives honest feedback on your process.

The pressurized basket is more forgiving and a useful training wheel for your first week. Once you've got your grinder dialed in, switch to the single-wall basket and don't look back.

The grinder is not optional

We cannot stress this enough: the Bambino Plus will expose your grinder. A cheap blade grinder or a grinder that can't reach espresso-fine settings will produce bad shots regardless of how well everything else is dialed in. Budget at least $150–200 for a grinder alongside this machine. The Baratza Encore ESP is our pairing recommendation.

04

Milk steaming

This is where the Bambino Plus genuinely earns the "Plus" distinction over the base Bambino. The auto-steam wand is one of the most useful features on any beginner machine we've tested.

Auto-steam

You set a texture level (1–4, from silky flat-white microfoam to thick cappuccino foam) and a temperature, drop the wand into the included pitcher, and press start. The machine handles aeration, heating, and stopping at your target temperature automatically. The results are consistently good — better than most beginners produce manually in their first few weeks.

Manual steam

The same wand also operates manually for those who want to learn the technique. It's a smaller, single-hole tip compared to prosumer machines, but it produces latte art-quality microfoam once you develop the feel for it. Expect 2–3 weeks of practice before your microfoam is consistently glossy.

The wand's auto-purge function activates after each steam cycle, clearing residual milk before it dries inside the tip — a thoughtful detail that extends the wand's life and keeps maintenance simple.

05

Build quality and design

The Bambino Plus feels substantially more solid than its price suggests. The brushed stainless exterior is the same material used on Breville's $1,000+ machines — not plastic with a chrome finish. Buttons have a positive click. The portafilter seats firmly with no wobble.

At 7.7 inches wide, it's genuinely compact for a semi-automatic machine. It fits comfortably under most kitchen cabinets.

A few practical notes

The water tank mounts at the rear, which means you need to pull the machine forward to refill it. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing before you decide where to position it on your counter. We recommend leaving 4–6 inches of clearance behind the machine.

The drip tray is removable and dishwasher-safe — clean it weekly. Coffee oils build up fast and make the tray look terrible within days if ignored.

Bambino Plus vs. De'Longhi Dedica Arte

The Dedica Arte costs $300 less and is a genuinely capable beginner machine. Its pressurized basket is more forgiving, which makes the learning curve less steep in the first few weeks. But that same pressurized basket limits your ceiling — you can't dial shots in as precisely, and the espresso quality plateaus below what the Bambino Plus can achieve. If $499 is out of reach, the Dedica Arte is a smart choice. If you can stretch to the Bambino Plus, do it.

Bambino Plus vs. Barista Express

The Barista Express has a built-in grinder, which sounds convenient. In practice, you're locked into that grinder forever — and it's decent but not great. A Bambino Plus paired with a Baratza Encore ESP gives you better grind quality at roughly the same combined price ($499 + $195 = $694 vs. $699). The Bambino Plus setup also lets you upgrade the grinder independently later, while the Barista Express doesn't.

07

Bottom line

The Breville Bambino Plus is our top pick for anyone serious about home espresso at the $400–500 price point. The 3-second heat-up eliminates one of the biggest daily friction points with home machines. The non-pressurized basket gives you the feedback you need to actually improve. The auto-steam wand removes the steepest part of the learning curve for milk drinks.

The one firm prerequisite: you need a good separate grinder. Budget $150–200 for one alongside this machine and the combined investment will outperform any all-in-one machine at double the price.

What to buy with it

Grinder: Baratza Encore ESP (~$195) — the ideal pairing at this price.

Scale: Timemore Black Mirror Basic Plus (~$55) — fast response, built-in timer.

Tamper: Normcore V4 54mm (~$39) — matches the Bambino Plus's 54mm portafilter exactly.
